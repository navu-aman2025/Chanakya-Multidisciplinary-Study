## A. Extended Notes on Key Theories

### A1. Saptanga Theory (Seven Limbs of the State)
The Saptanga model outlines the essential components required for a stable and functional state:
- **Swami** (Ruler)
- **Amatya** (Ministers)
- **Janapada** (Territory & People)
- **Durga** (Fortifications)
- **Kosha** (Treasury)
- **Danda** (Army & Enforcement)
- **Mitra** (Allies)

These elements form the backbone of Chanakya’s political science and administrative philosophy.

### A2. Mandala Theory (Circle of States)
Chanakya’s geopolitical model describes concentric circles of:
- Allies  
- Enemies  
- Neutral states  
- Middle powers  

This framework remains relevant in modern international relations, especially in regional diplomacy and strategic balancing.

## B. Additional Context on Economic Principles

### B1. Taxation Philosophy
Chanakya’s taxation system emphasizes:
- Progressive tax rates  
- Fair distribution of burden  
- Avoidance of excessive taxation  
- Protection of vulnerable groups  

His approach anticipates modern public finance principles.

### B2. Market Regulation
Chanakya advocates:
- Prevention of monopolies  
- Price stability  
- Anti‑fraud measures  
- State oversight of essential goods  

These principles align with contemporary regulatory frameworks.
