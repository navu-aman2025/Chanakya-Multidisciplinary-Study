# Limitations

While this study provides a comprehensive multidisciplinary analysis of Chanakya’s contributions, several limitations must be acknowledged. These limitations arise from historical constraints, interpretive challenges, and methodological boundaries inherent in studying ancient texts and comparing them with modern systems.

## 1. Variations in Translations of Primary Texts
The *Arthashastra* exists in multiple translations, each with differences in terminology, interpretation, and emphasis. These variations can influence how certain concepts—such as statecraft, economics, or diplomacy—are understood. Although multiple translations were consulted, complete uniformity in interpretation cannot be guaranteed.

## 2. Loss of Original Manuscripts
The original manuscripts of the *Arthashastra* are not available, and existing versions are reconstructed from later copies. This creates uncertainty regarding:
- Missing sections  
- Editorial modifications  
- Historical interpolations  

Such gaps may affect the accuracy of certain interpretations.

## 3. Cultural and Temporal Differences
Comparing Chanakya with Greek philosophers or modern governance systems involves significant cultural and temporal differences. Concepts developed in ancient India may not align perfectly with Western philosophical frameworks or contemporary administrative models. These differences limit the precision of cross‑cultural comparisons.

## 4. Contextual Constraints in Modern Application
While Chanakya’s principles remain relevant, applying ancient frameworks to modern systems requires adaptation. Factors such as:
- Democratic governance  
- Globalization  
- Technological advancements  
- Human rights frameworks  

were not part of Chanakya’s historical context. Therefore, modern applications must be interpreted cautiously.

## 5. Limited Availability of Secondary Literature
Although scholarship on Chanakya is growing, it remains limited compared to studies on Greek or Western political thinkers. This restricts the depth of comparative analysis and may lead to reliance on a smaller pool of academic sources.

## 6. Interpretive Bias in Multidisciplinary Analysis
The study integrates perspectives from economics, political science, sociology, and governance. While this enriches the analysis, it also introduces interpretive bias, as each discipline may emphasize different aspects of Chanakya’s work. Balancing these perspectives is inherently challenging.

## 7. Constraints of Visual Representation
Diagrams such as the Saptanga Theory, Mandala Theory, and Governance Flowchart simplify complex ideas for clarity. However, visual models cannot fully capture the nuance and depth of Chanakya’s frameworks, and may omit minor but relevant details.

## 8. Scope Limitations
The study focuses on major themes and does not cover:
- Chanakya’s literary contributions  
- Detailed military strategies  
- Ritualistic or cultural aspects  
- Minor administrative rules  

These omissions were necessary to maintain focus but represent areas for future exploration.
